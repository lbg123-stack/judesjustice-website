Equipment

Equipment that makes daily life safer and more comfortable, and what to ask before you get it. We do not sell anything, and we are not paid to list any product.

Getting equipment

  • Most specialist equipment starts with an assessment by an occupational therapist (OT) or physiotherapist. Ask social work or the person's NHS team for a referral.
  • Ask for the assessment and the decision in writing, including what was agreed and when it will be delivered.
  • Keep a record of every chase in My file. If it does not arrive, use the Chase a promise letter.

Hoists

A ceiling track hoist runs along a rail fixed to the ceiling, so the person can be moved between bed, chair, bath and toilet without a mobile hoist on the floor. Installing one usually needs an OT assessment and a check of the ceiling.

  • Ask which rooms the track should cover, and whether it will reach the toilet and bath.
  • Ask who services and inspects it, and how often.
  • Everyone who uses it needs moving and handling training. Add it to the training record.

Posture, head and neck support

Good positioning, day and night, protects breathing, digestion, comfort and body shape. Head and neck supports can help someone hold their head in a safe, comfortable position when sitting or travelling.

Always ask the person's physiotherapist or OT before buying head or neck support, as the wrong support can cause harm.

If posture changes, for example leaning to one side or a curve in the spine, ask for a wheelchair and seating review and a community physiotherapy review. Seating, splints, standing and moving and handling equipment should be looked at together.

Seating, including seat-to-stand chairs

Specialist seating, riser chairs and seat-to-stand chairs can help with comfort, posture and moving from sitting to standing. The wrong chair can cause pain or pressure sores, so ask an OT or physiotherapist to assess before buying.

Vehicles and adaptations

  • A wheelchair accessible vehicle (WAV) lets the person travel while sitting in their wheelchair.
  • With the higher mobility rate of disability benefits, you can lease a car or WAV through the Motability scheme. See Benefits.
  • Ask about the adaptations the person needs, such as a ramp or lift, wheelchair restraints, and space for oxygen, suction and a carer.

Standing and walking aids

Supported standing and stepping can help with bone health, digestion, breathing and joy of movement. Some devices let a person be supported upright by an adult while they take steps. Ask the person's physiotherapist whether one is safe for them.

Swimming

Most public pools require swimwear that contains incontinence. Specialist reusable swim pants and swimsuits are available for children and adults. Check the pool's rules before you go, and whether it has a Changing Places toilet (see Places and therapies).

Preparing food

If the person has a modified or blended diet, the texture must match the level set by their speech and language therapist. The IDDSI framework describes the levels. A powerful blender or food processor makes it easier to reach smooth textures safely.
